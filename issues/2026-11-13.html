2. SCIENCE AND REGULATION

Each item: grade, hype-vs-evidence tag, lens and desk, a short evidence summary, who it matters to, and its links. Items already reported in Digest #4 (Nov 9) are not repeated unless there is a new development.

2.1 FDA requires NAION warning on all GLP-1 receptor agonists (FDA, Nov 9, 2026)

Grade: B (regulatory fact; meta-analysis signal) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence; absolute risk small Lens: N/T · 06 MEDICAL, 11 RULES & RISK · Init. 8

FDA now requires every GLP-1 receptor agonist label to list non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), a sudden loss of vision from reduced blood flow to the optic nerve, under Warnings and Precautions. FDA's meta-analysis of 126 placebo-controlled trials (134,253 patients) found 15 probable cases, 10 on GLP-1 drugs and 5 on placebo: 6.4 versus 3.8 cases per 100,000 person-years. Background NAION incidence in people over 50 is 2.3–10.2 per 100,000. Ars Technica reports that FDA's observational comparison found no added risk against SGLT-2 inhibitors but a higher risk against DPP-4 inhibitors. FDA says a causal link has not been established.

Matters to: Personalized Medicine Advisor (GLP-1 client briefings); Client Communicator Reports (add 'sudden vision change: seek care' to GLP-1 notes); Init. 8 Hear & See Well.

Links: FDA safety communication · CNBC · Ars Technica

2.2 Rapamycin did not extend survival in 11,474 kidney-transplant recipients (AgeBeacon, November 2026)

Grade: B (large observational study; null result) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence cools 'rapamycin extends human life' marketing Lens: T · 06 MEDICAL · Init. 24

Over about 11 years of follow-up, 4,885 transplant recipients taking mTOR inhibitors (rapamycin-class drugs) were compared with 6,589 who were not. Median survival age was 64.6 versus 64.3 years, a difference that was not statistically significant (95% CI −1.6 to +2.0 years). Caveat: transplant patients take other immunosuppressants at doses unlike the low, intermittent schedules used for longevity, so this tests the drug class in a sick population, not the off-label longevity protocol.

Matters to: Advisory Board Manager (Kaeberlein and Attia have both discussed rapamycin); Evidence Diligence; Product Manager (rapamycin truth ranking).

Links: AgeBeacon

2.3 Four weeks of low-dose rapamycin raised brain blood flow in APOE4 carriers (pilot) (press release Nov 5; paper Sept 22, 2026)

Grade: C (single-arm pilot, n=23) Hype vs. evidence: Mixed; surrogate marker only Lens: N · 03 MIND, 06 MEDICAL · Init. 9

University of Missouri researchers (Ai-Ling Lin) gave 1 mg sirolimus daily for four weeks to 23 cognitively normal adults aged 45–65 (9 APOE4 carriers, 14 non-carriers). Cerebral blood flow rose by more than 15% in carriers, with the largest gains in women, but not in non-carriers. There was no placebo group and no cognitive outcome; the paper appeared in the Journal of Cerebral Blood Flow & Metabolism on September 22 and returned to the news with a November 5 release.

Matters to: Personalized Medicine Advisor (APOE4 clients ask about rapamycin); Multidomain Brain Health (Init. 9) watch-list; Evidence Diligence.

Links: ScienceDaily · Longevity Benchmark

2.4 Partial reprogramming turned back a blood-cell aging marker by up to 60 years in the lab (WIRED, Nov 11, 2026)

Grade: C/D (cell culture only) Hype vs. evidence: High hype risk Lens: T · 07 MEASURE · Init. 18

A German team led by Oliver Brüstle (study in Aging Cell) took blood cells from donors ranging from infants to a 101-year-old and partially reprogrammed them without making them fully pluripotent. Within two months one aging biomarker moved back by as much as 60 years, and rejuvenation continued even when cell division slowed. WIRED notes the mechanism is unclear and that blood cells and neurons age on different clocks, so results measured in blood may not translate to other tissues.

Matters to: Longevity Expert; Product Manager (screen 'decades younger' claims); Aging-clock dashboard (Init. 18).

Links: WIRED

2.5 Review of 350+ studies: protein and amino-acid restriction slows aging in models (ScienceDaily / Cell Press, Nov 10, 2026)

Grade: C (narrative review; mostly animal and mechanistic data) Hype vs. evidence: Headline outruns human evidence Lens: T · 06 MEDICAL · Init. 15

The Cell Press Blue review 'The hallmarks of protein and amino acid restriction in aging and longevity' pools more than 350 studies and points to methionine, isoleucine and valine as key levers in metabolism and lifespan in animal models. The authors say it does not establish that eating less protein extends human life and that needs depend on age and activity. Counterpoint: Attia's November 7 piece (reported in Digest #4) argues older adults need adequate protein to hold muscle. Suggested: no change to protein guidance for older clients.

Matters to: Mediterranean Table (Init. 15); Client Communicator Reports; Advisory Board (Attia).

Links: ScienceDaily · Attia, The protein paradox

2.6 65,028 accelerometer wearers: short, intense, intermittent bursts track with lower mortality (BMC Medicine, Nov 10, 2026)

Grade: B (large prospective cohort) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence (observational) Lens: N/T · 02 BODY, 07 MEASURE · Init. 6

In Germany's NAKO cohort (ages 19–74, median 8.0 years, 1,634 deaths), more activity volume was linked with lower mortality (HR 0.73, 75th vs 25th percentile), and each 1 mg increase in average acceleration with about 7% lower risk. Intensity of the most active minute was linked even more strongly (HR 0.58), and intermittent activity showed stronger links than continuous bouts (1-minute continuous HR 0.71; 120-minute HR 0.83). The authors do not claim causation.

Matters to: Gaming Guru (Strength & Steps Quest, Init. 6: reward short bursts, not only step totals); Longevity Dashboard (Init. 18).

Links: BMC Medicine (NAKO)

2.7 Genetic tendency to nap in midlife linked to dementia in 16,164 Swedish twins (Alzheimer's & Dementia, Nov 9, 2026)

Grade: C (genetic association, observational) Hype vs. evidence: Easy to misread as 'naps cause dementia' Lens: N · 02 BODY, 03 MIND · Init. 7, 9

Over about 16 years, each standard-deviation increase in genetic predisposition to midlife napping was associated with 11% higher dementia risk, possibly reflecting shared genetics with blood pressure and BMI. Reported napping in late life was associated with lower risk. The study shows association, not cause.

Matters to: Sleep Rhythm Coach (Init. 7: treat daytime sleepiness as a signal, not a target); Multidomain Brain Health (Init. 9).

Links: Alzheimer's & Dementia (PMC) · Aging Research News

2.8 Pandemic stress linked to faster epigenetic aging in adolescents (PNAS; ScienceAlert Nov 9, 2026)

Grade: C (observational; clocks disagree) Hype vs. evidence: Mixed; one generation only Lens: N · 01 ISOLATION · Init. 4, 20

Saliva samples from more than 1,000 people in Germany's TwinLife study (children about 8–10, adolescents 14–16, adults 20–29) taken before March 2020 and again in 2021–22 showed faster aging on three of four epigenetic clocks. The Max Planck Society reports that adolescents' perceived burden and loss of social contact were linked with the acceleration. Whether this leads to earlier disease needs follow-up.

Matters to: Connection Circle (Init. 4) and Intergenerational Play (Init. 20): social contact as a measurable aging factor; Psychology Health Expert.

Links: ScienceAlert · Max Planck Society

2.9 Five epigenetic clocks failed to predict general brain aging in 1,196 older women (Aging, release Nov 9, 2026)

Grade: B (cohort with MRI) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence tempers clock marketing Lens: T · 07 MEASURE · Init. 18

In the WHIMS cohort (women 65+ at enrollment, MRI about eight years later), none of five clocks was associated with general brain aging. GrimAge2 was associated with an Alzheimer's-pattern MRI signature, driven by its smoking pack-years methylation marker even after adjusting for self-reported smoking.

Matters to: Longevity Dashboard & Aging Clocks (Init. 18: keep clocks labelled research-grade); Evidence Diligence.

Links: Aging (journal) release

4. BEHAVIORAL TOOLS — COMPANIONS, STORY CONVERSATION, GAMIFICATION

4.1 Smart speakers cut loneliness in residents aged 80+ (BMC Psychology, published Nov 2, 2026)

Grade: C (quasi-experimental, cluster-allocated) Hype vs. evidence: Promising; design limits Lens: T · 04 BEHAVIOR TOOLS, 01 ISOLATION · Init. 2, 19

140 residents aged 80+ in six Chinese care facilities were allocated by facility to smart-speaker use or usual care for 12 weeks; 132 completed. Loneliness (UCLA short form) fell from 19.4 to 14.5 in the speaker group, a large effect (d = 1.02). The trial was registered retrospectively and was not randomized at the individual level.

Matters to: Psychology Health Expert; Daily Check-in Ritual (Init. 19): a low-cost voice pilot before glasses; TVV Shop Manager.

Links: BMC Psychology

4.2 AI soft toy 'Moflin' tested in German care homes (dpa via Yahoo News, Nov 2, 2026)

Grade: D (unpublished field study described in press) Hype vs. evidence: Anecdotal Lens: T · 04 BEHAVIOR TOOLS · Init. 23, 2

A Hamburg care home has trialled the furry AI companion since late 2025. An ILAG study placed 50 Moflins in five care homes; just over half of residents showed genuine interest, while some rejected it. Prof. Oliver Korn: "it's competing with a complete lack of affection."

Matters to: Pet & Animal Companionship (Init. 23); Companion & Story (Init. 2). Companion Corner lesson: offer, never impose.

Links: dpa via Yahoo News

4.3 New York companion-bot law S9051B restricts 'unsafe AI companion features' (DLA Piper alert, Nov 5, 2026)

Grade: B (enacted law) Hype vs. evidence: Hard rule Lens: O/risk · 11 RULES & RISK · Init. 2, 3

Signed October 28 and effective January 1, 2027, the law bans these features for minors and for any user who is not age-verified: claiming to be human or a character with emotions, framing a personal relationship or authority, sycophancy, unprompted emotion-based questions, and using health or personal information from earlier sessions. Penalties reach $25,000 per violation, and remedies include algorithm deletion. DLA Piper counts 11 other states with companion-bot laws.

Matters to: Companion design charter (Init. 2): its 'remember what matters' rule needs adult age assurance and consent; Trauma-Sensitive Design Standard (Init. 3); counsel review before any public launch.

Links: DLA Piper alert

5. PHYSICAL TOOLS — EXOSKELETONS, LIGHT SMART GLASSES, WEARABLES

5.1 Hip exoskeleton RCT: faster walking and more confidence in healthy 60–75-year-olds (Frontiers in Psychology, Nov 9, 2026)

Grade: C (small RCT, unblinded) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence, early Lens: T · 05 PHYSICAL TOOLS · Init. 10

60 adults were randomized (58 analyzed) to 8 weeks of Aider3S hip-exoskeleton walking (three 40-minute sessions a week) or control. Walking speed rose from 0.82 to 1.03 m/s versus 0.81 to 0.83 m/s; the 6-minute walk rose from 326.6 to 389.3 m. Self-efficacy and balance confidence also improved. Participants were not blinded and there was no power calculation.

Matters to: Physical Products Guru (Assist-Wear shortlist, Init. 10); Strength & Steps Quest (Init. 6) as a protocol template.

Links: Frontiers in Psychology

6. SOCIAL, ENVIRONMENTAL AND ECONOMIC LONGEVITY

6.1 Isolation and loneliness linked to worse health across 18 countries (BMC Medicine, Nov 10, 2026)

Grade: B (harmonized cohorts, n=94,122) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence (observational) Lens: N · 01 ISOLATION · Init. 2, 4

Pooling five cohorts of adults aged 50+ in 18 countries, both social isolation and loneliness were associated with worse physical, mental and self-rated health. The finding holds across very different welfare systems, which supports exporting connection programs.

Matters to: Connection Circle (Init. 4); Companion & Story (Init. 2); Sales Advisor (B2B evidence slide).

Links: BMC Medicine (18 countries)

6.2 FTC: reported fraud losses of adults 60+ topped $3 billion in 2025 (FTC report dated Oct 19; analysis Nov 5, 2026)

Grade: B (government data) Hype vs. evidence: Evidence; probably undercounted Lens: N/O · 10 MONEY · Init. 22

Reported losses rose from about $2.4 billion in 2024 to more than $3 billion in 2025, and investment-scam losses passed $1 billion (up 42%). Allowing for under-reporting, the FTC estimates the true cost at $13.3 billion to $119.5 billion.

Matters to: Financial Longevity & Fraud Shield (Init. 22); Capitalist CFO; companion scam-check prompts (Init. 2).

Links: FTC report (PDF) · Consumer Finance Monitor

11. FUSION LEADER TAKEAWAY

This week the evidence pushed back on the hype: GLP-1 drugs gained a vision-loss warning, rapamycin showed no survival gain in a large transplant cohort, and epigenetic clocks failed to track general brain aging. Meanwhile the tools for our priority initiatives became cheaper and more real: camera-free smart glasses from $349, a hip exoskeleton with RCT-measured gains in walking speed, and smart speakers that cut loneliness in residents aged 80+. Our edge is to build the Longevity Game and companion line on graded evidence and consent-first design, which New York's companion-bot law now makes a requirement rather than a nicety.
